4 On your iPhone: pair them & make the sticker

  1. Reliable route: AirDrop both files to the iPhone at the same time. Select the .JPG and the .MOV together (they share the same name — keep it that way) and AirDrop them in one go. They should arrive in Photos as a single Live Photo. On a Mac, you can also drag both files into the Photos app together and they will import as one Live Photo, then sync to your iPhone.
  2. About saving from the Files app. Saving both files from the Files app with Share → Save Image sometimes lands as two separate items — a still and a video side by side — instead of one Live Photo. If that happens, delete both and use the AirDrop or Mac Photos route above; that pairing route is the reliable one.
  3. Check it plays. Open it in Photos and long-press — it should animate, with the LIVE badge up top.
  4. Turn it into an iMessage sticker. In Photos, long-press the subject in the Live Photo and tap “Add Sticker” — or in Messages, open the + menu → Stickers → + and pick the Live Photo. Live Photo stickers stay animated in the chat.

Why two files?

A Live Photo isn't one file — it's a JPEG + a short MOV linked by an identical content ID stamped into both files' metadata. That stamp is the whole trick the paid apps charge for. This page writes it for free, right in your browser.

Good to know

The video is converted with a real QuickTime (MOV) encoder in your browser, so the .MOV is a genuine QuickTime file — H.264, with sound kept as AAC when the source has it. The first conversion downloads the encoder once (about 30 MB) and can take a little while on longer clips; keep the tab open while it works.
